# Live to Eat Food: Monetization, Ethics, Pricing & Platform Business Plan (October 2026)

Live to Eat Food can become a trusted, profitable regional food media business. The way to get there is to start with labeled paid content production and recurring restaurant retainers sold off Tina's existing social reach. That income should fund an owned website and Sunday newsletter that earn modest direct-sold, clearly labeled restaurant advertising in year one. Display ads, memberships and multi-market expansion should wait until real traffic data exists. The binding constraints are Tina's time (she is a full-time nurse practitioner), the small amount of sponsored inventory her audience will accept, and the fact that the website has no audience yet. Follower count is not the constraint.

## TL;DR

- **Best near-term revenue:** labeled sponsored Reels/TikToks, content production, and $750–$2,000/month restaurant retainers. At market-aligned prices this realistically grosses about $1,200–$5,500/month from social alone, limited by Tina's hours and a cap of roughly 1 sponsored post in 4. Published marketplace data puts the average food & drink creator sponsorship at $180 (Collabstr), and 80% of collaborations cost under $300. A creator with ~22K Instagram and ~14.8K TikTok followers and a concentrated local audience can justify above-average prices only by showing local reach analytics.
- **The website is a 12–24-month bet, not an immediate revenue source.** The prototype is a noindex concept with stand-in photos. Display networks need traffic it doesn't yet have: Raptive's October 16, 2025 blog says it will "require a minimum of 25,000 monthly pageviews," with 50% from top-tier countries below 100K. Restaurant ads should be sold as low-priced pilots with performance reporting, never as guaranteed visibility.
- **Trust is the product.** Follow the FTC Endorsement Guides (revised June 29, 2023) and the Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (effective October 21, 2024). Never sell ratings, rankings or "best of" inclusion. Label every paid placement. Keep the technology partnership separate from editorial control: Tina owns the brand, content and social accounts, and the developer owns or licenses the platform code with a revenue share on website-derived revenue only.

## Part 1 — Industry Patterns & Comparables

**How food creators earn (2025–2026):** Small and medium local creators earn mainly from (1) sponsored posts and hosted visits, (2) content production (photo/video assets restaurants use themselves, increasingly called UGC), (3) event hosting, and (4) recurring restaurant retainers. Collabstr reports UGC campaigns rose from 15% to 35% of its platform's engagements in 2025 while TikTok-specific campaigns fell 48%.\[16\] That trend favors selling *content production* rather than only *posts*. Larger creators add owned-media income (newsletters, sites, products).

**Why regional influence beats raw follower count:** A restaurant cares about diners who can drive there. 10,000 local views can be worth more than 100,000 national views. Advertisers measure this through audience location (city/DMA), saves and shares (intent signals), redemptions of unique codes, and reservation or foot-traffic lifts. Tina's pricing therefore needs a local-reach multiplier backed by analytics.

## Part 2 — Verified Pricing Guide

**Benchmarks (published) vs. recommendations (experimental):** The only robust published dataset is Collabstr (marketplace, skewed toward small creators). Use it as a floor. Tina's local concentration, video production and restaurant-specific outcomes justify pricing above marketplace averages *only when shown in analytics*.

**Pricing formula (customize once analytics arrive)**

> **Price = [Production cost + (Expected local impressions ÷ 1,000 × Local CPM)] × Usage multiplier × Exclusivity multiplier × Rush multiplier**, with a minimum floor.

| Input | Default assumption | How to calibrate |
|---|---|---|
| Production cost | Hours × $75–$125/hr (shoot, edit, captions, travel) | Track actual hours for 5 jobs |
| Expected impressions | Median views of last 10 comparable posts | From IG/TikTok insights |
| Local share | % of audience in Worcester County/Central MA | Platform audience location |
| Local CPM | $20–$40 per 1,000 *local* views (assumption, informed by local newsletter CPM claims of ~$50)\[25\] | Test and adjust by close rate |
| Usage rights | ×1.0 (organic only); ×1.3–1.4 for 90-day paid-ad/website use (Collabstr: +39%) | Per contract |
| Exclusivity | +15–25% per 30 days of category exclusivity | Rarely grant |
| Rush (<5 days) | ×1.25 | — |

*Worked example:* A Reel with 10,000 median views, 70% local, gives 7,000 local views × $30 CPM = $210. Add 4 hours × $75 = $300 production. Base price is about $510, which rises to about $690 with 90-day ad usage.

## Part 3 — Ethics: Visit Types & Hard Cases

| Model | Who pays | Disclosure | Rating allowed? | Platform rules |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Organic visit | Tina pays | None needed | Yes | — |
| Hosted/complimentary | Restaurant comps meal, no obligation | **Yes**, if she posts ("They comped our meal") | Yes, with disclosure | IG/TikTok: gifted product counts as branded content → label\[12\]\[13\] |
| Sponsored visit | Restaurant pays for deliverables | **Yes**, plus platform label | **No stars**; describe experience honestly | Meta Paid Partnership label; TikTok commercial content toggle; YouTube paid-promotion box\[26\]\[27\]\[28\] |
| Paid promotional feature | Restaurant buys a labeled feature | "Sponsored" | No | Website: label + rel="sponsored" links\[29\] |
| Sponsored listing | Directory placement | "Sponsored" badge, separate from editorial | No | Google: qualify paid links\[30\] |

**Hard scenarios: positions**

- **Tina dislikes a sponsored restaurant:** The contract buys *content production and distribution*, not opinion. Offer a choice: publish an honest labeled piece, or cancel publication with a kill fee (e.g., 50%) and deliver the raw assets for the restaurant's own use. Never publish fake enthusiasm. Under 16 CFR 465.2(a), influencers "could be liable" if they lie about their experience (FTC Q&A).\[31\]
- **Can a restaurant pay for a review without influencing its rating?** Not credibly. Paid pieces get no rating. Ratings exist only on organic or disclosed-hosted reviews.
- **Must she publish after a comp meal?** No, if no obligation was agreed. State "no guaranteed coverage" in writing beforehand.
- **Pre-publication approval?** Allow *factual* review (prices, hours, spellings, allergens) and brand-safety checks on sponsored content. Never allow opinion edits on editorial content.
- **Stars on sponsored content?** Never.
- **Paying for higher search placement?** Only in a clearly labeled "Sponsored" slot visually separated from organic results. Default sort and editorial rankings are never for sale.
- **Category sponsorship while being independently reviewed?** Yes, if the sponsorship is labeled ("Brunch guide presented by X") and X is excluded from that guide's ranked picks or assessed under the normal standard with a disclosure.
- **Demand to remove a negative review:** Correct factual errors promptly. Never remove opinion for payment or under threat. Note any update with a date. Suppressing negative reviews is a deceptive practice under the review rule's suppression provisions as applied to businesses, and paying to alter reviews triggers Yelp's Compensated Activity Alerts.\[31\]\[32\]
- **Buying raw content without an endorsement:** Yes. Content production/licensing is a clean product. If the restaurant posts it, the restaurant's ad must not imply Tina's endorsement unless she actually endorses it and the connection is disclosed.

**Restaurant outreach: introduction**
> Hi [Name], I'm Tina from Live to Eat Food. I've been eating my way around Worcester and posting about it for years. Besides my own independent reviews (which I always pay for unless I say otherwise), I offer a few paid services for local restaurants: short-form video and photos you can use on your own channels, clearly labeled sponsored posts, and event hosting. If any of that is useful, I'd love to send my rate card. And to be totally upfront: paying for a partnership never buys a review or a rating.

**Paid content creation offer**
> I can come in for about two hours, shoot 4–6 short videos and 15 edited photos of your dishes and space, and deliver them within a week for your Instagram, website and menus. It's $[X], plus the cost of the food we shoot. This is content for you to post; it doesn't include a post on my channels unless we add that separately.

**Hosted dining proposal**
> If you'd like me to come try the new menu, I'm happy to. Just know there's no guaranteed post. If I share it, I'll mention that the meal was complimentary, and I'll say what I honestly thought.

**Multi-restaurant campaign**
> I'm putting together a "Shrewsbury Street, stop by stop" series this fall. Sponsors get a labeled spot in the series and the newsletter. My picks for the ranked list stay my own, and sponsors are labeled separately.

**Independent vs. sponsored explanation**
> There are two ways you might show up on Live to Eat Food. Editorial: I chose to go, usually paid my own way, and say what I think. Sponsored: you paid for a labeled feature or content. Sponsored pieces never get my ratings and never appear in my "best of" picks as a paid placement.

**Audience disclosures**
- Complimentary meal: "This meal was comped by [Restaurant]. No one asked me to post, and these are my honest thoughts."
- Paid partnership: "Paid partnership with [Restaurant]. They paid me to make this video; the opinions are mine." (+ platform label)
- Sponsored video (spoken in first seconds): "This video is sponsored by [Restaurant]."\[53\]
- Affiliate link: "Some links here are referral links. If you book through them, I may earn a small fee at no cost to you."
- Sponsored website listing: "Sponsored: [Restaurant] paid for this placement. Tina didn't pick it, and it doesn't affect her reviews."
- Supplied products, no editorial control: "[Brand] gave me this to try. They didn't see or approve this post."
- Own business: "Full disclosure: Tinaesthetics is my own business."

**Commercial communications**
- *Rate-card introduction:* "Here's my current rate card. Prices include filming, editing and posting; food and extended usage rights are separate. Every paid post is labeled, and I can't promise a positive review — what I can promise is great content and honest, local reach."
- *Creative independence:* "You'll get to check facts (prices, hours, spelling, allergens) before anything goes live. The voice and opinions stay mine. That's why people trust my recommendations, and why your sponsorship works."
- *Sponsorship inquiry response:* "Thanks for reaching out! Are you looking for a sponsored post, content for your own channels, or event coverage? Quick heads-up: requests for an independent review go through my editorial inbox and aren't something that can be purchased."
- *Monthly package proposal:* "For $1,250/month (3-month minimum), you'd get one labeled sponsored post, four short videos and fifteen photos for your channels each month, plus a monthly report on views, saves and code redemptions."
- *Declining favorable-coverage demands:* "I'm not able to guarantee positive coverage or change a review in exchange for payment — it would break the trust that makes my audience worth reaching. I'm glad to correct any factual error right away."

## Part 6 — Website Monetization Comparison

The prototype's strengths are real. It has a "Where should we eat?" decision tool, video-as-menu pages, neighborhood maps along Route 9/I-190, a Sunday newsletter concept, and four honest labels: "Tina ate here," "Verified business," "Sponsored" and "Referral link." The promise that "Being listed is free, forever" is a trust asset. Weaknesses: it is `noindex`, uses Wikimedia stand-in photos, has unconfirmed addresses, and has no audience.\[54\] Original photography and confirmed data are prerequisites for launch.

**Display ads detail:** AdSense has no traffic minimum but pays little at low volume.\[55\] Journey by Mediavine is the on-ramp: Mediavine's own requirements page says it needs "a minimum of 1,000 sessions from Tier 1 countries… within a 30-day period," and it is reported at a 70% publisher share with the Grow plugin required. Mediavine's main tier requires "a minimum of $5,000 in annual ad revenue" rather than a session count. Raptive's blog (October 16, 2025) says "we'll require a minimum of 25,000 monthly pageviews to join Raptive," replacing the old 100,000 minimum, with 50% of traffic from the US/UK/CA/AU/NZ for sites under 100K. A Worcester-focused site will meet the geography test but not the volume test in year one. **Do not add ads until about 25K pageviews; they degrade UX and earn little before then.**

**Affiliates:** OpenTable's affiliate/partner terms are disclosed only on application. Third-party claims of $0.25–$1.00 per seated diner are unverified, and some trackers report no public program, so verify before building around it.\[56\]\[57\]\[58\] Practical Worcester options: restaurant gift-card referrals, regional food-tour and cooking-class operators (negotiate direct referral fees), and kitchen-product links via standard retail programs. Expect low revenue; the value is in usefulness.

**Memberships and discounts:** Restaurant discounts that members expect monthly erode restaurant margins and partner goodwill. Woo-pons works because discounts are voluntary, time-limited and restaurant-chosen. Keep it that way, and don't promise members guaranteed savings.

## Part 8 — Editorial & Advertising Policy (publishable)

> **How Live to Eat Food works**
> 1. **Tina's picks are never for sale.** Ratings, rankings and "best of" lists reflect only Tina's experience. No restaurant can pay to be included, ranked higher, or excluded.
> 2. **Who paid for the meal.** Tina pays for her meals unless we say otherwise, right on the page. Comped meals are labeled "Complimentary."
> 3. **Sponsored means paid.** Sponsored posts, placements and collections are labeled "Sponsored," never carry ratings, and appear visually separate from editorial picks. Sponsored restaurants may still appear in editorial lists only if Tina independently chose them, with the relationship disclosed.
> 4. **Sales and editorial are separate.** Whoever sells ads does not decide coverage. Tina has final say on editorial content, and advertisers never preview opinions.
> 5. **Verified vs. opinion.** "Verified business" means the owner confirmed hours, address and menu. It is not an endorsement.
> 6. **Corrections.** Restaurants can request factual corrections at [email]; we fix verified errors within 3 business days and note the date. We don't remove honest opinions on request or for payment.
> 7. **Updates and closures.** Reviews older than 18 months are labeled with the visit date; closed places are marked "Closed" and removed from active guides.
> 8. **Conflicts of interest.** Tina discloses businesses she owns or has a stake in (including Tinaesthetics), and doesn't review direct friends' or family's restaurants without saying so.
> 9. **Referral links.** Some links earn us a fee; they're labeled and never change what we recommend.
> 10. **Listings are free forever.** Paid options add features and labeled visibility, never rank or reputation.

**Non-negotiables in any deal (illustrative):**
- Tina retains the "Live to Eat Food" trademark, social accounts, her likeness, and her content (licensed non-exclusively to the site while the deal lasts).
- Platform code belongs to whoever is defined as owner, typically the developer under options 2/4, with Tina receiving a perpetual license to her site if the partnership ends.
- Subscriber and advertiser data is jointly controlled, with a portability clause. The list follows the brand on separation, because subscribers signed up for Tina.
- Editorial control stays 100% with Tina. The tech partner has no say in coverage.
- Expenses come off the top before revenue share; capital contributions are documented.
- Vesting or cliff for any equity (e.g., 4-year vest, 1-year cliff).
- Buyout at a predefined multiple (e.g., 1.5–2× trailing-12-month website net), with a right of first refusal.
- Dissolution terms cover code escrow, domain transfer, 90-day transition support, and non-disparagement.

**Platform vs. creator site:** A creator-specific site is cheaper and faster, and its value depends wholly on Tina. A scalable platform (multi-creator, multi-market) has enterprise value beyond her but needs capital, a sales team and creators in each market, and it risks diluting the personal voice that makes LTEF work. Build creator-first with clean architecture (structured place data, roles, labels) so the platform option stays open.

## Part 11 — SEO & Audience Growth

- **Content that can rank:** first-hand reviews with original photos and video, date-night and occasion guides ("Worcester birthday dinner," "Canal District before a WooSox game"), neighborhood pages (Shrewsbury St, Canal District, Park Ave, West Side), cuisine pages, and "new openings" pages updated monthly.
- **Avoid:** auto-generated pages for every restaurant without a visit. Thin directory pages compete poorly with Yelp and Google Maps and dilute quality signals. Show only places Tina has visited by default ("Only places Tina has been," as the prototype already does).
- **Structured data:** Mark up Tina's own editorial reviews of *other* businesses (Review with LocalBusiness/Restaurant itemReviewed). This is the third-party reviewer use case Google supports, unlike self-serving reviews.\[41\]\[43\]\[59\]\[60\] Never add review markup to sponsored pieces. Use rel="sponsored" on paid and affiliate links.\[40\]
- **AI search/answer engines:** concise, factual "Tina's verdict" summaries, visit dates, addresses and prices in visible HTML; consistent entity naming; an author page establishing Tina's identity and experience.
- **Year-one traffic plan:** Months 1–3: 30 cornerstone reviews + 6 guides, move the site off `noindex`, Google Search Console. Months 4–6: weekly newsletter; link in every video caption/bio ("full list on the site"); 2 guides/month. Months 7–12: refresh top 20 pages quarterly; partner with local outlets for guest lists; seasonal guides (patios, holiday, Restaurant Week). A realistic month-12 target is 8K–30K monthly pageviews and 1,500–5,000 subscribers (assumptions).
