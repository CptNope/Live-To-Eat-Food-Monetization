01 / THE OPPORTUNITY

Three businesses, one brand.

A diversified model lowers reliance on social-platform algorithms while creating real reasons for diners and restaurants to return.

01

Independent editorial

Tina's real dining experiences, original recommendations, neighborhood guides and videos. This is the credibility engine—never a pay-to-rank product.

02

Commercial content studio

Restaurants purchase clearly disclosed video, photography, promotional campaigns, events, asset licenses and ongoing retainers—not favorable reviews.

03

Owned discovery platform

Searchable places, maps, guides, saved lists, newsletter, enhanced restaurant tools and transparent local advertising on an owned website.

Local relevance beats vanity metrics.

A Worcester restaurant needs nearby people who might walk through its door. Verified Central Massachusetts reach, saves, clicks and repeat bookings matter more than a national follower count.

04 / TRUST & COMPLIANCE

Make the rules visible.

The research highlights FTC endorsement and review rules, Massachusetts consumer-protection law, platform disclosure policies and restrictions on compensated Google/Yelp reviews.

Independent

Organic review

Tina selects a restaurant, pays for the meal and gives an independent opinion or score under consistent editorial standards.

Disclose benefit

Hosted meal

A free meal or experience must be clearly identified when relevant to the content. Hosting does not purchase a favorable review.

Advertising

Paid promotion

Clear “Paid partnership” or “Sponsored” label, defined deliverables, factual review only, and no paid star ratings or editorial ranking.

Model disclosure: paid partnership

“Paid partnership with [Restaurant]. I was paid to create and share this content. The restaurant did not purchase a rating or control my opinion.”

Use platform branded-content tools and conspicuous wording in posts and videos, not a buried hashtag alone.

Five non-negotiable safeguards

  • No paid positive reviews or ratings.
  • No hidden complimentary meals or gifts.
  • No disguised sponsored rankings.
  • No perpetual commercial rights included by default.
  • No compensated Google Maps or Yelp review deliverables.

Policy and contract language should be reviewed by a Massachusetts attorney. Disclosure, privacy, CAN-SPAM, and platform rules vary by situation; this summary is not legal advice.

Contract decisions that matter more than the headline split

  • What revenue counts? Advertising, profile fees, paid guides, affiliates, newsletter deals; specify treatment of platform-driven social campaigns.
  • What stays Tina's? Existing social accounts, personal brand/likeness, existing content and unrelated sponsorships.
  • Who owns the technology? Source code, pre-existing developer components, licenses, domains, and access after termination.
  • Who pays operating costs? Hosting, tools, payment fees, production, sales, contractors and taxes.
  • Who makes decisions? Tina holds final editorial control; clarify sales, design and spending authority.
  • How does it end? Exit/buyout rights, term, review milestones, data portability and dispute resolution.
The research's default preference: retain Tina's existing creator assets separately; use a standard development fee or a clearly defined, time-limited platform-revenue share if development is discounted. Consider joint equity only if both parties commit comparable ongoing business-building effort.
07 / LAUNCH SEQUENCE

What to do over the next 12 months.

Prioritize paid learning, original content, repeat advertisers and measurable diner actions before adding complex platform features.

Days 1–30

Validate the business

  • Export 12 months of first-party social insights
  • Interview 10–15 restaurant owners
  • Publish editorial/disclosure policy
  • Finalize rate card and contracts
  • Sell 3–5 paid pilot campaigns
Days 31–90

Launch and measure

  • Launch owned-domain website
  • Curate 20–40 strong place pages
  • Add newsletter and click tracking
  • Document two campaign case studies
  • Convert 1–3 pilots to retainers
Months 4–6

Build repeat revenue

  • Expand to 50–100 useful place pages
  • Create Worcester search-intent guides
  • Sell direct newsletter/site sponsorships
  • Test free versus enhanced listings
  • Track advertiser renewals
Months 7–12

Expand carefully

  • Improve saved places and discovery
  • Test selective events and affiliates
  • Offer annual sponsor packages
  • Explore nearby markets already covered
  • Expand only if Worcester performs

The first real milestone: renewal.

Track median local reach, content-production costs, website-to-restaurant clicks, newsletter sign-ups and repeat sponsorship purchases. Sponsor retention is more useful than headline follower growth.

09 / WHAT THE THREE REPORTS ADD

Decisions hidden in the full research.

Beyond the executive brief, all three reports include operational details worth validating before launch. The following are research takeaways and recommendations, not final legal or financial conclusions.

Commercial operations and proof

  • Media kit: obtain 90-day or 12-month first-party reach, audience geography, typical Reel and Story views, and real campaign results before quoting premium reach.
  • Sponsor workflow: separate editorial tips from paid advertising inquiries; define deposits, deliverables, revisions, cancellation, usage rights and results reporting.
  • Licensing: price organic reposting, paid amplification, raw footage, exclusivity and extended terms separately.
  • Pilot validation: ask restaurants what leads and redemptions matter; measure renewals, menu clicks, referrals and coupon use.
  • Woo-pons: test clearly identified newsletter offers, track actual redemptions and disclose financial or affiliate relationships.

Website governance and launch

  • Corrections policy: date factual corrections; never change editorial rankings because an advertiser requests it.
  • Discoverability: use original content, useful local guides, descriptive restaurant pages, appropriate structured data and qualified sponsored links.
  • Privacy and accessibility: plan consent, data minimization, mailing-list unsubscribe, secure processing and usable mobile navigation.
  • Partnership boundaries: specify website-only receipts, direct expenses, code and content licenses, domain and email-data control, voting, exit and buyout procedures.
  • Merchandise and events: confirm fulfillment, refunds, tax and event/merchant obligations before offering paid products.

Research consistency checks — do not copy unverified numbers into a sales kit

  • Audience data conflict: Claude's October 2026 search snapshots show different Instagram figures from Gemini's estimates. Gemini's claimed 65–68% local audience share is not supported by the first-party analytics required elsewhere in the research.
  • Financial modeling issues: some Gemini pro-forma figures are inconsistent with their own assumptions, and partner distributions should not automatically be counted as ordinary operating expenses. Rebuild forecasts from actual inputs before using them for decisions.
  • Source traceability: embedded AI citation tokens such as filecite…, numbered citations and image markers may not resolve outside the original research sessions. Verify and replace key claims with working primary-source links.
  • Legal nuance: FTC endorsement disclosure, the Consumer Reviews Rule, Massachusetts consumer protection and data-security requirements have different triggers; the reports are not legal opinions. Recheck current rules and have counsel review contracts and public policy.
  • Design and partnership status: v2 awaits feedback; a 70/30 website split is a proposal, not an agreed or independently validated valuation.
